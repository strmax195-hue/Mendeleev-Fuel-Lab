# Dynamic engine calibration and WLTC validation

Stage 3 tests a Stage 2 survivor on three specified engines. For each compatible architecture the public record contains 25 warm operating points, a −30 °C start assessment, four drive-cycle phase balances, and catalyst and sump temperature histories. A **Golden Candidate** must pass on at least one architecture. Its dossier identifies precisely which architectures cleared the gates. Road controls and privately computed previews are excluded from the count.

**[STAGE-3 CERTIFIED]** is the project's label for computational qualification. No vehicle has been type-approved by this project. An OEM ECU release, a guaranteed knock-free operating envelope, a safe oil-drain interval, and a regulatory WLTP result require measurements. The downloadable university protocol names the control, the instruments, and the comparisons needed to test a prediction.

## Knock, ignition lag and timing

The solver follows the slider–crank volume from −150° to +150° in 2° steps. A polytropic exponent of 1.35 describes compression/expansion. A midpoint heat impulse deposits a bounded Wiebe heat release between successive volumes. The pressure–volume integral, less pumping and speed-dependent friction, yields brake work and torque. The model assigns 89% of LHV to heat release in the cylinder for this calculation. End-gas temperature follows its compressed pressure trajectory. A search over timing targets CA50 near 8° after TDC while prioritising the model limits.

Spark ignition uses the Livengood–Wu integral `I = ∫ dt/τ`. The ignition-delay correlation is `τ = 0.01768 (ON/100)^3.402 p_atm^−1.7 exp(3800/T)` in seconds. At each crank step `dt = dθ/(6 rpm)`. The solver integrates reciprocal delay to 98% mass burned and locates the first unity crossing within a step. A cell crossing unity fails. This is a reduced end-gas model with correlation transfer uncertainty for oxygenates. The reported crossing angle and remaining margin allow direct comparison with a pressure trace. [Livengood and Wu](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0082-0784(55)80047-1) · [Douaud and Eyzat](https://doi.org/10.4271/780080) · [Correlation and limitations for fuel blends](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016236115009667)

SI flame speed uses a pressure/temperature power law with mixture-strength and water-dilution terms. Bore, compressed laminar speed and mean piston speed set the turbulent flame travel time. CA90 later than 45° ATDC flags late heat release and a need for exhaust-valve temperature measurement. The finite injector window also bounds achievable equivalence ratio. A lean value below 0.75 is flagged. Local stratification, wall quenching and individual exhaust-valve temperatures require experiments. The GDI low-speed/high-load region (≤2,000 rpm and ≥14 bar BMEP) triggers independent LSPI review. Stochastic oil-droplet pre-ignition cannot be certified from an end-gas integral. [Flame-speed temperature and pressure dependence](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-2180(82)90127-4) · [Sequence IX engine test](https://doi.org/10.4271/2018-01-1808)

The diesel delay follows the Hardenberg–Hase correlation in the CFX formulation. The cetane-dependent activation energy is `618840/(CN+25)` J/mol. The pressure-dependent term uses absolute bar, and pressures at or below 12.4 bar contribute zero reaction progress. Integration begins at injection and the unity crossing defines modeled pressure-rise onset. Injection duration and a mixing tail set burn duration. Delays above 25° or onset later than 12° ATDC block qualification. Diesel mixing-controlled combustion has its own lag field and no premixed laminar-speed reading. [Hardenberg and Hase](https://doi.org/10.4271/790493) · [CFX theory equations](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v252/en/pdf/Ansys_CFX-Solver_Theory_Guide.pdf)

Each cell reports required fuel mass `m_air/(AFR × λ)`, open time from injector mass flow proportional to `√ρ`, and commanded pulse including latency. The **pulse percentage** uses 100% for the architecture's matched control. A positive **timing offset** means earlier spark or injection in degrees BTDC. At −30 °C the solver first checks the Stage 2 phase and lean-volatility bounds. For SI it estimates wall-film release over a 0.60 s crank window. For feasible compression ignition it uses an unheated 200 rpm compression trajectory and a 14% wall-loss scenario. An unattainable start has no numeric enrichment command.

## Oil exposure and qualification

Combustion water follows the elemental inventory. The model estimates the portion of blow-by water that condenses into a 4 kg sump and fuel reaching the sump through wall wetting. Temperature-dependent evaporation removes water and fuel. The acid-equivalent scenario includes an upper sulfur assumption of 10 mg/kg and an empirical condensate contribution. Source and decay balances run at one second. Repeated cold cycles carry sump contaminants forward and check exposure peaks within each cycle. The engineering limits are 8 g water, 120 g fuel and 8 g KOH-equivalent reserve depletion. A 10,000 km scenario service cap applies. These values are sensitivity assumptions. The resulting **model drain ceiling** is a test-planning quantity. The vehicle-approved interval remains unknown until OEM limits and used-oil analyses are available. The thresholds are project research preferences. Each winning formula receives a **1,000.000 g** gravimetric worksheet, mass allocations to the nearest milligram, matched control targets at 2,000 rpm and 60% load, and Markdown and JSON protocols. ## Independent computation

The regular three-stage publication command is `python -m engine.stage2_publish --output .lab --batch 24` for existing callers. A Python 3.10+ worker can precompute an admitted formula or up to eight contiguous catalogue IDs offline. Publication consumes contributions only when their candidate actually clears Stage 2. Private projections and reference fuels cannot create public champions. The worker replays the entire calculation and checks SHA-256 on verification. Packets contain no user, machine, account or submission timestamp fields. The Stage 2 scientific identity has a pinned digest alias for the CLI refactor. Earlier weather sectors retain their original model ID.

python cli/local_worker.py --stage 3 --candidate ai95100 --output calibration-reference.json
python cli/local_worker.py --verify calibration-reference.json
python cli/local_worker.py --stage 3 --start 300 --count 2 --output contributions/calibration-0300-02.json
